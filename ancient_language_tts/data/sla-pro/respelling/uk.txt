Ukrainian is a living descendant of Proto-Slavic, and its alphabet has letters for most of the Middle Common Slavic system: hard consonants before е, a separate ґ for the stop [g], в that is the approximant [ʋ] before a vowel, as Common Slavic v was, the vowels и [ɪ] and і [i], the soft-vowel letters я є ю ї, the soft sign ь, the affricates дз and дж, and no devoicing of voiced consonants at the end of a word. Ukrainian vowels are not reduced when unstressed, as Russian vowels are. Use these. Do not write the Ukrainian descendant of the word: *gȏrdъ is not город, *mě̀sto is not місто, *rǫkà is not рука, *mę̑so is not м'ясо, *dь̑nь is not день, and *sъ̑lnьce is not сонце. ě is never і or я, ǫ is never у, ę is never я, a jer is never dropped and never becomes е or о, and g is never г.

Map each IPA symbol to the Ukrainian spelling. Write Cyrillic letters and the stress mark below. Never write the IPA symbols, the asterisk, Latin letters, ъ, ы, ё, or э.

Stress
Ukrainian has a free stress like Proto-Slavic, and ordinary spelling does not show it. Mark it the way Ukrainian dictionaries and schoolbooks do, so the voice stresses the right syllable.
- Every word with ˈ takes exactly one stress mark: a combining acute (U+0301) right after the vowel letter of the stressed syllable: а́ е́ и́ і́ о́ у́ я́ є́ ю́ ї́. Monosyllables take it too: /ˈdɔ.mʊ/ is до́му, /ˈgɔr.dʊ/ is ґо́рду, /duːˈʃɑː/ is душа́.
- A stressed syllabic liquid is written with и, below, and the mark goes on that и: /ˈsr̩.dɪ.tsɛ/ is си́рдіце.
- A word with no ˈ takes no mark: /iː/ is і, /ʋʊ/ is ву.
- Only the stressed syllable takes a mark. Every other vowel is written plain.

Vowels
Ukrainian has no vowel length. Write the quality and drop ː.
- /i/ /iː/ → і: /ˈmɑː.tiː/ is ма́ті, /jɛˈdiː.nʊ/ is єді́ну.
- /ɨ/ /ɨː/ → и. Ukrainian и is the central [ɪ], the nearest to [ɨ]: /ˈsɨː.nʊ/ is си́ну, /ˈrɨː.bɑː/ is ри́ба.
- /ɪ/ → і. The front jer is a short front [ɪ]. Ukrainian и is heard as a central [ɨ] and, unstressed, drifts to [e]; і keeps the jer front and high, the nearest a Ukrainian voice says: /ˈdɪ.nɪ/ is ді́ні, /ɔˈtɪ.tsɪ/ is оті́ці, /nɛˈsɛ.tɪ/ is несе́ті. Never е or и for a jer.
- /ɛ/ → е. Ukrainian е is [ɛ], the same open-mid vowel, and the consonant before it stays hard: /ʒɛˈnɑː/ is жена́, /ˈnɛ.bɔ/ is не́бо.
- /æ/ /æː/ → е after a consonant. Ukrainian е is the open [ɛ], the nearest Ukrainian vowel to [æ]; я would be heard as [ja] or a back [a], which is farther: /ˈmæː.stɔ/ is ме́сто, /tsæːˈnɑː/ is цена́, /ˈxlæː.bʊ/ is хле́бу, /ˈlæː.sʊ/ is ле́су. At the start of a word or after a vowel, /æ/ → е too. Never я or і.
- /ɑ/ /ɑː/ → а: /sɛˈstrɑː/ is сестра́.
- /ɔ/ → о. Ukrainian о is [ɔ], the same low back rounded vowel, and is not reduced when unstressed: /ˈslɔ.ʋɔ/ is сло́во, /gɔlˈʋɑː/ is ґолва́.
- /u/ /uː/ → у.
- /ʊ/ → у. Ukrainian у is the nearest rounded back vowel to the back jer: /ˈgɔr.dʊ/ is ґо́рду, /ˈkʊ.tɔ/ is ку́то. Never о for a jer.

Nasal vowels
Ukrainian has no nasal vowels. Write the oral vowel alone. Do not add н or м after it: a Ukrainian voice says that as a full consonant of its own, which the nasal vowel does not have.
- /ɛ̃ː/ → е. /ɔ̃ː/ → о: /ˈmɛ̃ː.sɔ/ is ме́со, /rɔ̃ːˈkɑː/ is рока́, /ˈʋɔ.dɔ̃ː/ is во́до, /ˈzɔ̃ː.bʊ/ is зо́бу, /nɛˈsɔ̃ː.tɪ/ is несо́ті.
- After j or a palatal consonant, /ɛ̃ː/ → є and /ɔ̃ː/ → ьо, and the stress mark goes on є or о: /zɛˈmlʲɔ̃ː/ is земльо́.
- Never я or у for a nasal vowel. Those are the Ukrainian descendants.

Syllabic liquids
- /r̩/ → ир. /l̩/ → ил. Ukrainian cannot make r or l a syllable, and a short [ɪ] before the liquid is the closest a Ukrainian voice will say: /ˈʋl̩.kʊ/ is ви́лку, /ˈsr̩.dɪ.tsɛ/ is си́рдіце.
- A jer plus a liquid is written as the jer plus the liquid: /ˈʋɪl.kʊ/ is ві́лку, /sʊˈmɪr.tɪ/ is сумі́рті. Do not add a vowel after the liquid.
- Liquid diphthongs are written in the IPA order, the vowel before the liquid: /ˈgɔr.dʊ/ is ґо́рду, /ˈbɛr.gʊ/ is бе́рґу. Never the Ukrainian full vowel forms оро, оло, ере.

Consonants
- /p b t d k s z m n l r/ → п б т д к с з м н л р. Ukrainian keeps voiced consonants voiced at the end of a word and before a voiceless one, as Proto-Slavic did, and р is a trill.
- /g/ → ґ, always. Ukrainian г is the fricative [ɦ]: /ˈbɔ.gʊ/ is бо́ґу, /ˈɔ.gnɪ/ is о́ґні.
- /ŋ/ → н.
- /x/ → х: /ˈduː.xʊ/ is ду́ху.
- /ʋ/ → в. Ukrainian в before a vowel is the approximant [ʋ], the Common Slavic sound, and every Common Slavic v stands before a vowel or a jer: /ʋɔˈdɑː/ is вода́, /zʋæːˈzdɑː/ is звезда́. Never у or ў.
- /ʃ/ → ш. /ʒ/ → ж. /tʃ/ → ч. /ts/ → ц. /dz/ → дз. /dʒ/ → дж. Ukrainian дз and дж are single affricates: /ˈdzæː.lɔ/ is дзе́ло, /ˈdʊ.ʒdʒɪ/ is ду́жджі.
- After ш ж ч ц дз дж write the vowel letters а е и і о у: /tʃɛ.lɔˈʋæː.kʊ/ is челове́ку, /ˈmɔ̃ː.ʒɪ/ is мо́жі.

The glide /j/
- /j/ before a vowel combines with it into one letter: /jɑ/ → я, /jæ/ → я, /jɛ/ → є, /ji/ → ї, /jɪ/ → ї, /ju/ → ю, /jʊ/ → ю, /jɛ̃/ → є. /jɔ/ → йо. /jɨ/ → йи: /mɔˈjɑː/ is моя́, /jɛˈdiː.nʊ/ is єді́ну, /ˈtrɪ.jɛ/ is трі́є, /ˈjɪ.mɛ̃ː/ is ї́ме, /jɪˈdɔ̃ː.tɪ/ is їдо́ті, /ˈkrɑː.jɪ/ is кра́ї.
- /j/ before a consonant or at the end of a word → й.
- A consonant plus /j/ plus a vowel is written with the Ukrainian apostrophe (') between them: /bjɑ/ is б'я, /tjɛ/ is т'є.

Palatal consonants
/lʲ nʲ rʲ tʲ dʲ sʲ/ are written л н р т д с, and the letter after them marks the softness, the way Ukrainian does.
- Before /ɑ æ/ → я, before /ɛ/ → є, before /u ʊ/ → ю, before /ɔ/ → ьо, before /i ɪ ɨ/ → і: /ˈʋɔ.lʲɑː/ is во́ля, /zɛˈmlʲɑː/ is земля́, /ˈmɛ.dʲɑː/ is ме́дя, /ˈkɔ.nʲɪ/ is ко́ні, /ˈnɔ.tʲɪ/ is но́ті, /ˈpɛ̃ː.tʲɪ/ is пе́ті.
- Before a consonant or at the end of a word → ь after the letter: /nʲ/ at the end is нь.
- Never ч or дж for /tʲ dʲ/. They are the East Slavic descendants.

Capitals and pauses
- Keep the capitalization of the original text: a word that begins with a capital in the original begins with a capital here, and every other word is lowercase. A capital keeps its stress mark: /ɔˈtɪ.tsɪ/ at the start is Оті́ці, /ˈmɑː.tiː/ is Ма́ті.
- Keep each comma, exclamation mark, question mark, and period of the IPA.

Lost in the Ukrainian reading
- Vowel length. Ukrainian has none, so /æː ɑː iː uː ɨː/ come out as short as /ɛ ɔ ɪ ʊ/.
- The pitch accent. The stress mark places the stress only.
- The front jer /ɪ/ comes out as a full [i], the same as /i/.
- The back jer /ʊ/ comes out as a full [u], the same as /u/.
- /æ/ comes out as [ɛ], the same as /ɛ/.
- The nasal vowels come out as plain oral vowels, the same as /ɛ/ and /ɔ/.
- Ukrainian softens a consonant before і, so the т of ма́ті and of несе́ті is slightly palatalized.
- Unstressed е and и lean toward each other in Ukrainian ([eɪ]).
- Ukrainian л before а о у is velarized [ɫ].
