The voice is an Italian text-to-speech model. It applies Italian letter-to-sound rules and knows nothing about the source language. Write only the spellings listed here. The pair rules say which spelling stands for which sound. If a pair rule adds an onset or a nucleus, that addition is allowed for that language only.

Shape
- The line is ordinary text. A space separates words. A word is one string of letters. Do not put a hyphen inside a word.
- The vowel of a syllable marked ˈ takes the accent: à è é ì ò ó ù. In a diphthong the accent sits on the first vowel: ài òi àu éi éu ùi. The rest of that word is lowercase. Do not capitalize a syllable to mark stress.
- A syllable marked ˌ is written like any unstressed syllable: no accent and no capital.
- A word with no stress mark has no accent.
- The first letter of a sentence is a capital. No other letter is a capital. The accent stays on the stressed vowel: Gàllia èst, not GÀLLIA and not GÀ-llia.
- A diphthong is two vowel letters in one word. No apostrophe, and no hyphen.
- Keep a period, comma, question mark, or exclamation mark that the IPA uses as a pause. No other punctuation, no asterisks, no digits, no IPA symbols, no quotation marks.
- Do not copy the original orthography. A result that happens to be an Italian word is fine when the tables spell it that way.

How a syllable is built
- Onset, then the nucleus, then the coda, with no extra letter. Do not add a hyphen.
- The nucleus is one vowel letter, or one diphthong from the list below.
- Put consonants around the nucleus. qu before the nucleus ai is quai, one syllable. u before the vowel ì is uì, one syllable.

Nucleus spellings
- a à = the vowel of pane. à is that vowel, stressed.
- e = an unstressed e, as in the first vowel of vedere
- é = the stressed vowel of perché
- è = the stressed vowel of caffè
- i ì = the vowel of vino. ì is that vowel, stressed.
- o = an unstressed o, as in the first vowel of colore
- ó = the close o of bótte, a barrel, stressed
- ò = the open o of però, stressed
- u ù = the vowel of luna. ù is that vowel, stressed.
- ai ài = the diphthong of mai, one syllable
- oi òi = the diphthong of poi, one syllable
- au àu = the diphthong of pausa, one syllable
- ei éi = e followed by i, one syllable
- eu éu = e followed by u, one syllable
- ui ùi = u followed by i, one syllable. This u is the vowel, not the glide.

Consonant spellings
- b d f l m n p r t, each with its ordinary Italian value
- k = the consonant of kilo, including before e and i and at the end of a syllable
- g before a, o, u, or a consonant = the consonant of gatto
- gh before e or i = that same consonant. The letter h appears only as the second letter of gh.
- ghli = g plus l before i. The sequence gli is the palatal of figlio, so it is never that onset.
- gu = the onset of guerra, one onset
- qu = the onset of quanto, one onset. The letter q appears only as the first letter of qu.
- s at the start of a word, next to a consonant, or at the end of a word = the consonant of sole
- ss between vowels, the first s at the end of one syllable and the second at the start of the next = that same voiceless consonant, long
- a single s between vowels = the voiced consonant of rosa
- The letter i before a vowel in the same word is the glide of ieri. The letter u before a vowel in the same word is the glide of uomo. Neither glide is its own syllable. In uù the first u is the glide and ù is the vowel. Do not insert a hyphen to split them.
- r is a trill.
- n before k, g, or qu is the nasal of anche.
- The sequence gn is the palatal of gnomo. Do not write it.
- A geminate is a doubled letter inside the word: annus for a long n. Do not invent a geminate the IPA does not have. Do not split it with a hyphen.
- The letters c, j, w, x, y, and z never appear. h never appears except inside gh.

Sounds Italian cannot hold
- Vowel length, a nasal vowel, aspiration, a front rounded vowel, a dark l, and a short voiceless s between vowels are not separate letters. Use the substitute the pair rules name. Do not add a syllable in order to imitate length.
- Open and close e and o exist only in a stressed syllable. An unstressed e is e, and an unstressed o is o.
