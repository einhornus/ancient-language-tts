Stage. Late West Saxon, about 900 to 1000 AD, the teaching pronunciation of introductory Old English. c before a front vowel is /tʃ/. g before a front vowel is /j/. Do not read the text as modern English and do not read it as Middle English.

Spelling of the input
- A macron marks length. Where the input omits macrons, restore the dictionary quantities and transcribe those quantities.
- þ and ð are the same phoneme.

Vowels
- a /ɑ/, ā /ɑː/
- æ /æ/, ǣ /æː/
- e /e/, ē /eː/
- i /i/, ī /iː/
- o /o/, ō /oː/
- u /u/, ū /uː/
- y /y/, ȳ /yː/
- ea /æɑ/, ēa /æːɑ/
- eo /eo/, ēo /eːo/
- ie /iy/, īe /iːy/. At this date those are still diphthongs. If the spelling has already been updated to y or ȳ, use /y/ or /yː/.

Consonants
- p t b d m n w have their ordinary values. r is a trill /r/. l is /l/.
- c is /tʃ/ before a front vowel (i, e, æ, y, ea, eo, ie), when it is written ċ, and in word-final position after a front vowel (ic is /itʃ/). Otherwise c is /k/. cc follows the same split, geminated.
- g is /j/ before a front vowel and when it is written ġ. g is /ɣ/ between back vowels. g is /g/ at the start of a word before a back vowel or a consonant. cg is /dʒ/.
- ng is /ŋ/ plus the g that the rule above would give.
- sc in a native word is /ʃ/. In a Latin loan, sc is /sk/ when the cluster is still the Latin one.
- h at the start of a word is /h/. After a front vowel it is /ç/. After a back vowel it is /x/.
- hl, hr, hn are /l̥ r̥ n̥/. hw is /ʍ/.
- f and s are /f s/ at the start or end of a word and next to a voiceless sound, and /v z/ between voiced sounds.
- þ and ð are /θ/ at the start of a word and next to a voiceless sound, and /ð/ between voiced sounds.
- cn, gn, wl, wr are pronounced as written. A doubled consonant is a geminate.

Stress
- Stress the first syllable of the lexical word.
- The prefixes ge-, be-, and the verbal for- are unstressed, and so is ne. The stress then falls on the root. A noun in for- stresses the prefix.
- Put ˈ before the stressed syllable.
