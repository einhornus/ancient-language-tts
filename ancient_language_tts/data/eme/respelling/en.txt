The voice is a modern Irish English voice. Write modern English spelling wherever a word still sounds the same, and respell it wherever the 1600 sound differs from today's sound. A word whose pronunciation is essentially the same keeps its modern spelling. A word with a changed sound is respelled, even when the change is small, so the voice does not fall back on today's pronunciation. When English letters cannot write the 1600 sound exactly, write the nearest approximation given below.

The original text is the starting point
For this pair the instruction not to copy the original spelling means this: do not copy old spellings such as vnto, doe, beene, or loue, and do not copy a word whose IPA differs from its modern sound. Take the word as it is spelled today: vnto is unto, doe is do, beene is been, loue is love. Then compare its modern pronunciation with the IPA, sound by sound.
- Every sound is listed under "Keep" below: write the modern spelling, letter for letter. the, man, not, now, night, here, horse.
- Any sound is listed under "Respell": keep the letters of the sounds that did not change, and replace only the letters of the changed sound with the spelling given. love /ˈlʊv/ is loov, sun /ˈsʊn/ is soon, day /ˈdɛː/ is deh, meat /ˈmeːt/ is met, her /ˈhɛr/ is hair.
- Do not use the phonetic spellings ih, ae, or eye for a sound in the Keep list. eh is the FACE and MEAT spelling only, and aw is the THOUGHT spelling only. thuh, maen, nawt, and hihz are wrong. the, man, not, and his are right.

Keep: the modern spelling already gives these
- PRICE /əɪ/: the voice cannot say the central start of the 1600 vowel, and any respelling of it comes out garbled (thuy is heard as twee). Keep the modern spelling: mind, night, time, my, by, I, eye, thy, thine, silence, fire, besides.
- MOUTH /əʊ/: for the same reason, keep the modern spelling: now, how, thou, out, house, sound, our, hour, pronounced, outrageous.
- GOAT /oː/: keep the modern spelling of go. The voice's vowel of go is the nearest it has; aw, the vowel of law, is heard as the vowel of not or of father, so never write aw for GOAT. go, so, though, soul, own, home, stone, noble, nobler, arrows, window, borrower, cold, cloak, holding, glorious. Where today's word has another vowel, write o with a final e, oa, or a final o: gone /ˈgoːn/ is goan, shone is shown, host is oast. A final /oː/ in a respelled word is o: Horatio /həˈrɛː.sɪ.oː/ is Horesseeyo.
- FORCE /oːr/: more, door, force, hoarse.
- TRAP and BATH /a/: man, cat, hand, shall, ran, path, last, after, dance, father, and.
- LOT and CLOTH /ɔ/: not, God, soft, off, often, on, rotten, philosophy. THOUGHT /ɔː/: all, law, walk, talk, saw. of is the exception below.
- FOOT words /ʊ/ that have /ʊ/ today: good, foot, put, book, could, should, would, full, woman.
- FLEECE /iː/: see, be, thee, meet, speech, piece, field.
- KIT /ɪ/ and DRESS /ɛ/: sit, wind, his, him, bed, head, dead, breath, any, said, dreamt.
- GOOSE /uː/ where today's word has /uː/ too: do, too, food, moon, through.
- The due set /ɪu/ where today's word has /juː/: due, new, few, tune, music, dispute.
- happY /ɪ/: a final y or ie stays: merry, city, victory, weary, brevity.
- NEAR /iːr/, SQUARE /eːr/ and /ɛːr/, START /ar/, NORTH /ɔr/, CURE /uːr/: here, fear, there, where, bear, spare, fair, care, compare, art, hard, horse, short, for, lord, poor, sure. Irish English is rhotic and keeps horse and hoarse apart. The short vowel of NORTH has no closer spelling, so horse stays.
- lettER /ər/ in an unstressed syllable: winter, river, harder, father, honour.
- Unstressed syllables with /ə/ or /ɪ/: the, a, about, compare, temperate, discontent, infinite, garment, given, madness. to is the exception below.
- Consonants: r after a vowel, wh /ʍ/, th, ng and ng-g, silent gh, and the silent l of walk, half, could, and would stay as written today. kn is the exception below.

Respell: these sounds changed
- FACE /ɛː/: the voice glides today's vowel of name into ee, and the 1600 vowel is a pure long vowel. Write the vowel of bed, which has no glide. Drop the letters that spelled the modern vowel: a with a final e, ai, ay, ei, ey.
  - At the end of a word, write eh: day /ˈdɛː/ is deh, May is Meh, pray is preh, they is theh, grey is greh, say is seh.
  - Everywhere else write e, never eh: the voice reads eh inside a word as some other vowel. In a closed syllable write e alone: state /ˈstɛːt/ is stet, take /ˈtɛːk/ is tek, name is nem, made is med, rain is ren, date is det, shake is shek, frailty /ˈfrɛːl.tɪ/ is frelty, strained is strend. A final s, f, or l after e is doubled: face /ˈfɛːs/ is fess, lace is less, tale is tell. Before a vowel or before a consonant that starts the next syllable, write e and double that consonant, so the e stays short and stressed: able /ˈɛː.bəl/ is ebble, nature /ˈnɛː.tər/ is netter, Horatio /həˈrɛː.sɪ.oː/ is Horesseeyo, outrageous /əʊtˈrɛː.dʒəs/ is outredjus, neither /ˈnɛː.ðər/ is nether, wayfaring is wefferin.
  - Before r the word stays: care, fair, compare.
- The MEAT set /eː/ where today's word has the vowel of see: the same spelling as FACE, eh at the end of a word and e everywhere else. The voice has no glide-free vowel nearer to it. sea /ˈseː/ is seh, meat /ˈmeːt/ is met, speak /ˈspeːk/ is spek, east /ˈeːst/ is est, heat /ˈheːt/ is het, increased /ɪnˈkreːst/ is increst, lease /ˈleːs/ is less, please /ˈpleːz/ is plez, reason /ˈreː.zən/ is rezzen, creature /ˈkreː.tər/ is cretter. great /ˈgreːt/ is gret, break is brek, steak is stek. Before r the word stays: there, where, bear.
- STRUT /ʊ/, in words that have the vowel of cup today: write oo, the spelling of book and good, which is the 1600 vowel. sun /ˈsʊn/ is soon, but is boot, cut is coot, come is coom, son is soon, much is mooch, rough /ˈrʊf/ is roof, tongue /ˈtʊŋ/ is toong, love /ˈlʊv/ is loov, above is aboov, lovely is loovly, another /əˈnʊ.ðər/ is anoother, until /ʊnˈtɪl/ is oontil, unto /ˈʊn.tʊ/ is oonto, something /ˈsʊm.θɪn/ is soomthin. A doubled consonant after oo is written once: suffer /ˈsʊ.fər/ is soofer, summer /ˈsʊ.mər/ is soomer, buds is boods. troubles /ˈtrʊ.bəlz/ is troobels, so the voice does not read it as troubles. Words spelled oo today with the vowel of cup take ew: blood /ˈblʊd/ is blewd, flood is flewd. FOOT words that have /ʊ/ today stay.
- oi with /əɪ/: write the PRICE spelling of today, i with a final e or y: join /ˈdʒəɪn/ is jine, point is pint, boil is bile, toil is tile, loins is lines, poison /ˈpəɪ.zən/ is pyzen. With /ɔɪ/ the word stays: boy, joy, employ, voice, noise, choice.
- A final -y or -ie with /əɪ/ in rhyme: write ye, as in dye and rye. memory /ˈmɛ.mə.rəɪ/ is memorye. wind /ˈwəɪnd/ in rhyme is wynd.
- to /tə/: write ta. The voice says to and tuh with the vowel of too. to be is ta be, to take is ta tek. A stressed to /ˈtuː/ stays to. into stays.
- of /ɔv/: write ov. The voice says of with the vowel of cup, and ov keeps the vowel of not. of York is ov York, a sea of troubles is a seh ov troobels.
- NURSE in a stressed syllable has three vowels, and none of them is today's. Write the vowel with h, then r, and drop the letters that spelled the modern vowel: er, ear, ir, ur, or.
  - /ɛr/: write air, the vowel of fair, which the voice keeps as e before r. her /ˈhɛr/ is hair, were is wair, mercy /ˈmɛr.sɪ/ is mairsy, serve is sairve, heard /ˈhɛrd/ is haird, earth /ˈɛrθ/ is airth, learn is lairn.
  - /ɪr/: write ihr. bird /ˈbɪrd/ is bihrd, first is fihrst, sir is sihr, third is thihrd, birth is bihrth.
  - /ʊr/: write uhr. turn /ˈtʊrn/ is tuhrn, hurt is huhrt, cursèd /ˈkʊr.sɪd/ is cuhrsid, word /ˈwʊrd/ is wuhrd, work is wuhrk, world is wuhrld.
  - /ar/ in clerk, sergeant, merchant: write ar. clerk is clark, merchant is marchant.
- /a/ after w, wh, or qu where today's word has the vowel of not or of law: write ah. what /ˈʍat/ is whaht, was /ˈwaz/ is wahz, want is wahnt, wash /ˈwaʃ/ is wahsh, swan is swahn, quality /ˈkwa.lɪ.tɪ/ is quahlity. An unstressed /wəz/ stays was.
- /a/ with no l in palm words: palm /ˈpam/ is pam, calm is cam, balm is bam, alms is ams, psalm is sam.
- /ɪu/ where today's word has /uː/ with no y sound, or the ch or j sound: write eeoo, two vowel letters the voice says one after the other. Never y before it: ryoo is read as rye, and tyoo runs together into ch. true /ˈtrɪu/ is treeoo, rude is reeood, blew /ˈblɪu/ is bleeoo, flew is fleeoo, juice is jeeooce, suit is seeoot, juliet /ˈdʒɪu.lɪ.ɛt/ is jeeooliet, fortune /ˈfɔr.tɪun/ is forteeoon, virtue /ˈvɪr.tɪu/ is vihrteeoo, statue is stateeoo, verdure /ˈvɛr.dɪur/ is vairdeeoor.
- your and you're /jɪur/: write yewr. The voice reads your and yoor with the vowel of for.
- -ture with /tər/: write ter, and keep the stressed vowel's value. nature /ˈnɛː.tər/ is netter, creature /ˈkreː.tər/ is cretter, venture is venter, picture is picter.
- -sure with /zər/ or /sər/: write zer or ser after a doubled consonant. pleasure /ˈplɛ.zər/ is plezzer, measure is mezzer, treasure is trezzer. sure and sugar stay.
- -tion, -sion, -cion, -cious, -tial, -tient with /sɪ.ə/ or /zɪ.ə/: write see or zee, then y and the vowel, and keep the stressed vowel's value. nation /ˈnɛː.sɪ.ən/ is nesseeyun, occasion /ɔˈkɛː.zɪ.ən/ is okezzeeyun, passion /ˈpa.sɪ.ən/ is passeeyun, gracious /ˈgrɛː.sɪ.əs/ is gresseeyus, patience /ˈpɛː.sɪ.əns/ is pesseeyence. Never sh or zh.
- /sj/, /zj/, /rj/, /lj/ where today's word has a separate i or e syllable: write the consonant, y, and the vowel. nation /ˈnɛː.sjən/ is nessyun, glorious /ˈgloː.rjəs/ is gloryus, juliet /ˈdʒɪu.ljɛt/ is jeeoolyet.
- /tj/ and /dj/: write tee or dee, then the vowel, with no y. ti, ty, and tiu are all read as ch. question /ˈkwɛs.tjən/ is questeeun, Christian /ˈkrɪs.tjən/ is Cristeeun, soldier /ˈsoːl.djər/ is soldeeer.
- Unstressed -ing with /ɪn/: drop the g. darling /ˈdar.lɪn/ is darlin, holding is holdin, being is bein, wayfaring is wefferin, trippingly is trippinly. A stressed -ing stays: sing, king, thing.
- -ed with /ɪd/ where today's word has no syllable there: write id. cursèd /ˈkʊr.sɪd/ is cuhrsid, belovèd /bɪˈlʊ.vɪd/ is beloovid. -ed with /t/ after a consonant today spelled ch, sh, or ss may stay: pinched, pressed.
- An apostrophe word: spell its IPA with no apostrophe. heav'n /ˈhɛvn/ is hevn, o'er /ˈoːr/ is ore, e'en /ˈiːn/ is een, 'tis /ˈtɪz/ is tiz, lov'd /ˈlʊvd/ is loovd, in't /ɪnt/ is int. th' /ð/ is th, joined to the word the IPA joins it to.
- kn with /kn/: write cn, so the voice says the k. knight /ˈknəɪt/ is cnight, knee is cnee, knew is cnew, knave /ˈknɛːv/ is cnev, know /ˈknoː/ is cnoe. gn and wr stay.
- A silent h in a French loan where today's word says the h: drop it. humble /ˈʊm.bəl/ is oomble, herb is urb, host is oast, hospital is ospital. hour, honest, honour, and heir already have no h.
- Any other sound that differs from today's word: respell only that sound, with the spelling it has in the commonest English words, or the nearest approximation.

Writing the line
- Each word is one unbroken string of letters. Keep the word boundaries of the IPA.
- Keep the capitalization of the original text: a word that begins with a capital in the original begins with a capital here, and every other word is lowercase. Names keep their capital: York, Juliet.
- English has no stress accent. Stress is not marked. Drop ˈ and the syllable dots.
- Drop apostrophes: summer's is soomers.
- Keep the pause marks of the IPA: comma, period, question mark, exclamation mark.
