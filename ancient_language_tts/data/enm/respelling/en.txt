Do not apply the Great Vowel Shift. /iː/ is ee, so a Middle English tyme is teemuh, not teyemuh. /uː/ is oo, so hous is hoos, not hows. /x/ and /ç/ are pronounced.

Vowels
- /a/ /aː/ → ah
- /ɛ/ /ɛː/ → eh
- /eː/ → ey
- /ɪ/ → ih
- /iː/ → ee
- /ɔ/ /ɔː/ → aw
- /oː/ → oh
- /ʊ/ → uh
- /uː/ → oo
- /ə/ → uh, and a final /ə/ is its own syllable
- /ai/ → eye
- /au/ → ow
- /ɔu/ → oh, the whole nucleus. /knɔu/ is knoh, one syllable.
- /iu/ → yoo, the whole nucleus. /niu/ is nyoo, one syllable. yoo is allowed here.
- /ɛu/ → ew
- /ɔi/ → oy

Consonants
- /p b t d k g f v s z m n l r w h/ → the same letters
- /j/ → y
- /θ/ /ð/ → th
- /ʃ/ → sh
- /tʃ/ → ch
- /dʒ/ → j
- /ŋ/ plus /g/ in the same syllable → ng. Across a syllable break, /ŋ.g/ → ngg: /ˈjʊŋ.gə/ is yuhngguh
- /x/ /ç/ → kh
- /ʍ/ → hw
- /kn/, /gn/, and /wr/ keep both consonants.
- A geminate, the same consonant on both sides of a syllable break, is a doubled letter: /ˈsʊn.nə/ is suhnnuh.
